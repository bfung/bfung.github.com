California's Money, Past and Future

One timeline, from actuals to bond maturities

California's General Fund over time, the debt already locked in, and what the Nov 3, 2026 ballot would change if a measure passed. Check boxes to combine scenarios — the chart, the table, and the summary below are all computed in your browser from the same JSON file, so they cannot drift apart.

Nov 3, 2026 ballot — scenarios are hypothetical “if passed”, NOT results. Results are not yet known. Every proposition figure on this page is a scenario, not an outcome. Future note (not implemented now): after voting finishes, these choices will later be disabled and actual results baked into the baseline.

Debt service is the outflow. Principal is a memo. Bond proceeds are not revenue. Debt service (principal + interest) already sits inside General Fund expenditure — it is never stacked on top of expenditure as an extra outflow. Principal repaid is a memo, and is also the decline in outstanding debt. Bond proceeds go to a bond fund for capital purposes, not to the General Fund operating budget, so they never appear on the revenue line.

What this is — and what it isn't

This is one timeline with two panels sharing one fiscal-year axis. Panel A shows annual flows ($B per year): General Fund revenue and expenditure, and the surplus/deficit gap between them. Panel B shows end-of-year stocks ($B): bonded debt outstanding (GO + lease-revenue) as one series, with unfunded pension/OPEB liabilities as separate anchor diamonds — never merged into bonded debt, never connected into a line. Stocks and flows are never plotted on one axis, and a stock is never added to a flow.

It is not a forecast, and it is not the whole of California government. Special funds (~$95.1B spending in FY2026-27), federal funds (~$187.2B, mostly Medi-Cal pass-through), local government finances, and bond-fund proceeds spending are outside the General Fund series. Counting bond proceeds as revenue and debt as a liability would double-count the same dollar, so proceeds never appear as revenue here.

Two baselines — read the labels, do not average them

DOF-based baseline (the scenario-math default). An illustration holding FY2026-27 levels nominal, except the Prop 30/55 expiration drop from FY2031-32: baseline revenue falls by the $10B/yr midpoint (range $5–15B) because those rates expire at end-2030 under current law. Baseline expenditure in projection years is the non-debt base ($242.798B = FY2026-27 expenditure $251.477B minus FY2026-27 gross debt service $8.679B) plus that year’s DAR baseline debt service (sold bonds + intended sales). Baseline balance is revenue minus expenditure. This is labelled an illustration, NOT a forecast — LAO and the Governor disagree by tens of billions even for FY2026-27, so any single long baseline is illustrative by construction. Projection runs to FY2057-58 because that is when bond maturities end: sold GO bonds end FY2056-57, sold lease-revenue bonds end FY2050-51, and Treasurer intended-sales debt service ends FY2057-58.

LAO outlook baseline (balance/deficit only). From the LAO November 2025 Fiscal Outlook, a current-law baseline, not a prediction: FY2026-27 −$17.7B, FY2027-28–FY2029-30 −$35.0B/yr, and it STOPS at FY2029-30 — it is not extended to 2057. The FY2026-27 figure is the outlook’s “budget problem” / anticipated deficit, not a Chart A operating balance, so it is not directly comparable to the DOF baseline balance for the same year. No revenue/expenditure split is shown for the LAO series because the JSON encodes balance only, and scenario math never uses it.

Prop 40 — a band, a synthesis central line, and money that is not General Fund money

Central $65B — SYNTHESIS, not a published estimate: midpoint of Jaros & Rauh's face-value range after observed departures ($59–72B); before litigation discount; excludes missing billionaires and control-weighting. Low $30B is Jaros & Rauh’s litigation-weighted expected value (face value × a 0.50 survival weight — a convention, NOT a court forecast; their published range is $28–36B). High $100B is the Galle/Boll benchmark ($98–104B, rounded). The LAO itself gives no point estimate — only “tens of billions”, spread over several years. Blue Sky’s $116.2B headline and $151.4B sensitivity top are NOT used: the headline applies no avoidance haircut, and the top adds speculative uplifts.

Prop 40 revenue is NOT General Fund money: it is exempt from Prop 98, Prop 2, and the Gann spending limit, and goes to the 2026 Billionaire Tax Reserve Fund — 90% health, 10% education/food assistance. It is therefore plotted as a separately labelled Reserve Fund revenue band/line (“Reserve Fund revenue — NOT General Fund revenue”) on the same $B axis, and it is never added to GF revenue, GF expenditure, or the GF balance. Its Reserve Fund spending memo is shown separately: $13.0B/yr central = $11.7B health + $1.3B education/food. Appropriation caps of $22.5B + $2.5B = $25B/yr are non-binding on the $13B/yr instalment path, but would bind on a $65B lump sum in a single year, stretching spending past 2031.

Fiscal-year mapping straddle (flagged in the JSON): the triangulation memo’s instalment years are calendar 2027–2031 (tax due with 2026 returns in 2027, five equal instalments). This file maps calendar instalment year Y to the fiscal-year label ending June 30 of the same calendar year — 2027→FY2026-27, …, 2031→FY2030-31 — because returns are filed in spring of the instalment year, inside that fiscal year. The first instalment therefore falls in the FY2026-27 Governor’s-proposal baseline year, not in the first full post-passage year.

Footnote only — never baked into the $65B central total: if ALL taxpayers elect instalments, the central all-instalment path with deferral charges is $13.0 / $16.9 / $16.0 / $15.0 / $14.0B = $74.9B (effective rate 5.00% → 5.75%). The lump/instalment mix is unknown. Ongoing drag is a SEPARATE memo line, never netted: $0.5–3.5B/yr income-tax range (LAO low end to Jaros & Rauh high end); Walczak’s $3.53–4.49B/yr all-taxes figure is footnoted separately because it covers all state taxes including sales and spillover, not income tax only. Undiscounted break-even on $65B: ~19 years at $3.5B/yr, ~65 years at $1B/yr. FTB administration (LAO: tens of millions/yr for several years, paid from the new revenues) is noted, not netted.

Prop 3 — why the balance barely moves

Prop 3 makes permanent the higher income-tax rates from Prop 30 (2012) / Prop 55 (2016), which otherwise expire at end-2030. It has no effect FY2027-28–FY2030-31. From FY2031-32, the DOF baseline already drops by the $10B/yr midpoint — so checking Prop 3 restores that $10B/yr of revenue and the $10B/yr of program spending it funds (about 40% to schools/community colleges, the rest to other programs/reserves, on LAO’s split; Prop 98 / BSA pass-through is NOT separately modelled, so these are gross deltas). Flow levels rise; the balance barely moves. Presenting it as a pure balance improvement would be wrong.

Scenario bonds — totals are derived, sales are an assumption

LAO gives annual ranges and durations for Props 1 and 38, but no nominal totals and no sale calendar. The totals on this page are derived arithmetic from the LAO midpoints, and the sales are the APPROVED stated assumption of even spread over FY2027-28–FY2031-32, each tranche repaid as level annual payments over the LAO term starting the year after sale. Prop 1: $0.55B/yr steady state over 25 years, derived nominal total $13.75B, implied rate 2.6162% (derived, NOT LAO-stated). Prop 38: $0.55B/yr over 20 years, derived nominal total $11.00B, implied rate 2.7181% (derived, NOT LAO-stated). Early years are below $0.55B while sales ramp. A different sale calendar or rate would change the yearly shape, though not the authorization.

Unissued bonds — memo only, not debt

Authorized but unissued GF-supported bonds at June 30, 2026: $42.461B ($37.794B GO non-self-liquidating + $4.667B lease-revenue). Of that, the Treasurer’s 2-year intended-sales plan (FY2026-27–FY2027-28) covers $16.911B of principal, with $30.154B lifetime debt service estimated as of Aug 1, 2026. The remaining ~$25.55B has no sale plan and no dated series (memo only). Largest unissued balances: Prop 4 (2024) $9.670B, Prop 2 (2024) K–12 $7.150B, Prop 1 (Mar 2024) behavioral health $5.158B, stem cell (2020) $4.028B. Authorized but unissued bonds are NOT debt owed — never add them to outstanding debt or debt service until sold.

Gaps and limits — what is not drawn, and why

  • All-funds revenue: no verified annual series — shown nowhere, not backfilled. All-funds expenditure is verified only FY2024-25–FY2026-27 (table above).
  • General Fund debt-service history before FY2025-26: not verified (only DAR 2025-26 gross $8.14B and 2026-27 $8.679B are in the file). The table shows “—” for earlier years; do not infer it from secondary summaries.
  • BSA / general-purpose reserves: anchors only (table above). No single-vintage complete annual BSA series was verified, and a BSA figure from one vintage must never be added to a Chart A SFEU figure from another.
  • Bonded-debt history before June 30, 2025: not verified — it would require pulling each year’s DAR snapshot one by one. Panel B therefore starts at the FY2024-25 anchor ($80.77B) and FY2025-26 ($81.787B), then breaks at FY2026-27 (null in the JSON) before the sold-bond projection from FY2027-28.
  • Final FY2025-26 actuals: not in July 2026 Chart A. FY2025-26 is enacted/estimate and FY2024-25 is revised; a future Chart A vintage will move these rows. July 2026 Chart A rows must not be spliced with other vintages.
  • Remaining ~$25.55B unissued bonds: memo only, no dated series — assigning a sale year would invent a schedule.
  • Prop 1 / Prop 38 sale calendars and nominal totals: not in LAO. Sales are the stated even-spread assumption; totals are derived arithmetic, labelled as such.
  • Prop 37 repayment schedule and borrower-repayment inflows: not in LAO. Memo issuance is an assumption; repayments are note only.
  • Unfunded liabilities: anchors only (table above). CalPERS state-share figures for June 30, 2025 and OPEB for June 30, 2025 were not verified in this pass and are not used.
  • LAO baseline beyond FY2029-30: null by design. Do not extend it to 2057; the DOF construction is the only baseline running to FY2057-58, and it is an illustration, not a forecast.
  • Unissued-GO discrepancy (~$1.35B): the research memo’s DAR total GO unissued $39.14B vs Appendix A sum $38.200B, consistent with commercial-paper treatment but not confirmed to a line item. This file uses the clean $42.461B GF figure and flags the discrepancy rather than resolving it silently. The DAR cover-letter “over $44B” counts GO commercial paper and self-liquidating bonds and is not used as the GF figure.
  • Prop 40 unverified items: Dec 31, 2026 valuations; FTB residency rulings for named departures; whether courts uphold the Jan 1, 2026 date; control-weighted valuation; missing-billionaire count; lump/instalment mix. The $30B/$65B/$100B band is therefore conditional, with LOW confidence on any point estimate.
